Interactive Board Game

RFID-triggered board inspired by the Amphawa Floating Market

1RFID sensor (RC522)
4Actuator types: servo, LED, speaker, vibration
3Weather states, 3 sound loops
~10 APeak supply load

The problem

Teenagers’ growing screen dependence is linked to shorter attention spans and weaker social skills. Traditional board games help face-to-face engagement, but they struggle to hold the attention of tech-oriented teens.

The solution

A Raspberry Pi 5 scans weather cards and triggers real-time physical responses on the board: synchronized audio, LED objectives and servo-driven umbrella mechanisms.

Card scan→RC522 (SPI)→Raspberry Pi 5→ ServoLEDsSpeakerVibration

The Pi looks up each card’s UID against the weather card set and drives all four output channels in parallel. Audio loops until the next card is scanned, so the board always reflects the current round’s weather. A rain card rotates a paper umbrella to reveal a safe score spot, and only the LEDs for the scanned weather light up to guide players to high-scoring areas.

Debugging story: electrical noise

When the LEDs shared a buck converter and ground with the vibration motors, the motors stalled and the RFID reader stopped responding. The team traced it to shared power and ground, then cut the high-current, purely aesthetic LED strip and kept four LED groups. Recommended next fix: an isolated RFID supply with a capacitor, and separate LED and motor rails.

Playtest: three seniors and one 5th-grade student. The seniors praised the mechanics and sensory elements, and the younger player stayed engaged because the environment changes every round. Built with the Industrial Design group, who designed the market scene and umbrellas.

  • Raspberry Pi 5
  • RC522 RFID
  • PCA9685 servo driver
  • Servo motors
  • 2N2222 transistors
  • Buck converters
  • 10 A switching PSU
  • USB speaker

Smart IoT Water Bottle

Tracks how much you actually drink, and how good the water is

3Sensors: load cell, TDS, temperature
2,000 mLDaily intake goal tracked
2Alert channels: LINE and Blynk
4Tester groups, including company staff

The problem

Students and office workers often lose focus to poor hydration. Few people track their intake, and existing smart bottles need flat-surface calibration and can’t verify what was actually drunk.

The solution

An ESP32 with a load cell and HX711 amplifier measures consumption (1 g ≈ 1 mL), TDS quality and temperature. Data appears on an onboard LCD, syncs to Blynk IoT over Wi-Fi, and drives personalized LINE bot reminders.

Load cell + HX711TDSDS18B20→ESP32→ LCDBlynkLINE bot

Notification logic

  • An update after every drink, showing the amount consumed
  • An hourly reminder with total drunk and water remaining
  • A congratulation at 2,000 mL, then no more alerts that day
  • Automatic reset after 23:59 for a new cycle

Why it differs from commercial bottles

My literature review found commercial bottles often need recalibration and a flat surface, can’t tell drinking from spilling, and give no raw data or long-term storage. This prototype shows raw readings on its own display and in the app, and adds water quality and temperature alongside intake.

ClariU

An explainable AI detector for spam and phishing messages

97.0%Precision (target ≥95%)
94.0%Recall (target ≥90%)
95.5%F1 score (target ≥92%)
1.8%False positive rate (target <3%)

The problem

Many spam filters are black boxes that return a label without saying why. That makes them hard to trust, especially when real messages are flagged by mistake or clever phishing slips through.

The solution

Built at an AI Innovation Hackathon by The Stem Girlies, ClariU is a web tool that checks SMS, email and chat text. An optimized XGBoost / Logistic Regression pipeline is paired with LIME, so it shows the exact words driving each decision.

Features: a model performance dashboard comparing four models, spam weight per word, a customizable decision threshold, and sample prompts for quick testing.

Result

Placed 25th out of 157 submissions, and was praised for its ethical use of AI and clear use of ML for explainability.
